How we work

Mercury-Redstone Research looks for security vulnerabilities in widely used software, most of it open source, and reports each one privately to the people who maintain it. This page describes the work and how we go about it.

What we look for

We study software that many people depend on, most of it open source, and look for flaws that undermine its security. Our findings fall into two main areas:

  • Memory safety. Use-after-free, out-of-bounds access, uninitialized-memory disclosure and stack exhaustion in programs written in C and other low-level languages. We find these with fuzzing and sanitizer-instrumented builds, and trace each one back to the exact line of source at fault.
  • Access control. Authentication and authorization flaws that let one user reach another user's data, or an unauthenticated request reach something that was meant to be protected.

We confirm before we report

We report only flaws we have reproduced. For each one we build a proof of concept that triggers the flaw on a current build, save the evidence, identify the root cause in the source, and, where we can, include a suggested fix. We test only software that we build or install, and run, ourselves: never a live service and never anyone else's machine.

We report privately, through the stated channel

We disclose each finding privately to the maintainers or vendor first, through the channel they ask researchers to use, and we keep the details confidential until a fix or an advisory is published. The full terms — our deadline, how we credit, and when we publish — are set out in our disclosure policy.

What we have published

Once a fix is out, we publish an advisory. Everything we have published so far is listed on our advisories page, and our source repositories are on GitHub.
